Something about him intrigued me. In a world that values wealth and success more than anything else, seldom does one find a multi-millionaire founder at his prime walking away from all of it. The question that perplexed me was why would someone renounce and give away everything that I aspired for. The incident made me question societal assumptions and definitions of happiness and success that have become our norm. Curious, I grabbed his memoir – “If Truth Be Told: A Monk's Memoir” to find answers to some of my questions.

As I read the book, each page brought a new question, a new wonder, and a fresh awe at his belief, determination and courage. The last sentence of the book has stayed with me ever since – “I have given you my truth. Go, discover yours.”

Hindsight has great power – it allows us to explain things that we otherwise cannot. It makes us find reasons for things that could have happened purely out of randomness. So take what I am going to say next at your own peril, as the sentence has had a profound impact on my journey ever since. It furthered my insatiable quest to pursue the ultimate in ways I could not have fathomed at that time.

Merely reading why one would walk away from everything that modern society defines as success doesn’t sink in easily. As Morpheus told Neo in The Matrix – “there’s a difference between knowing the path and walking the path”. While I understood the argument, I had not walked a single step of it. After all, ideas of annual vacation, dining out on weekends, comfort of modern homes and cars, rising up the corporate ladder are too ingrained in our system. So much so that we start losing ourselves in the process and identifying with everything around us. How often do we introduce ourselves with our professional titles or ask someone we just met about what they did for a living?

Modern society starts to condition us as soon as we arrive. What else explains parents buying pink for girls and blues for boys? A child cannot even see colors until around fourth week after birth. So why all the fanfare, if not for unconscious conditioning that makes the parents act in a certain manner, which in turn starts conditioning the little one right from onset? In the guise of education, mannerisms and civility, we provide standard answers to everything that a child must discover. Throughout our lives, these readymade answers and methods become our way of being. And, slowly but surely we kill the joy of playful seeking with our pre – defined set of standards, principles and values.

All through, we pay a hefty price unknowingly – the price of never asking.

But isn’t enquiry, the very basis of uncovering the truth? Truth the way it is - “Satya”, not the way it has been told to us. Remember, when we quietly accept, we still choose. And every choice we make takes us either closer to the truth or steers us away from it.

Grace has its way that is beyond my current comprehension. A simple line from a book steered me to question my long held beliefs. Beliefs that defined who I thought I was and what I did. It set me on a path that has enriched my journey where the pain of ignorance is much greater than the vulnerability of admitting I don’t know. As the line said – “Go, discover yours”, I continue to seek and find answers in every aspect of life. I find it amazing that whether it is investing, scientific discovery or spirituality, all require us to see the truth as it is.

Through this musing, I have no answers to hand over. Years later the sentence is still working on me, and I have learned not to hurry it. I don't know if I have found anything, but I have stopped mistaking borrowed answers for my own. Now, I simply ask – “Why am I doing this? Does it take me closer to the truth?”
